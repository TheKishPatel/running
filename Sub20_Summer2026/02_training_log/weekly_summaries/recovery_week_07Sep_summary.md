## The ankle — the week's real content

**Left medial ankle, retromalleolar. Onset Mon 7 Sep, the day after the half.** Working hypothesis
**tibialis posterior tendinopathy**, with the race as the mechanism and two cycle commutes inside the
muscle-damage window as the reason it did not settle.

**Trajectory, which is the reassuring part:**

| Day | State |
|---|---|
| Mon 7 | Onset, day after a 21.1 km race |
| Wed 9 (day 3) | **Painful enough to force working from home** |
| Sat 12 (day 6) | **Full day of walking pain-free; e-bike pain-free** |
| Sun 13 (day 7) | Tightness on ten hops; **ran later the same day** |

**A stress reaction does not walk clean on day 6 and it does not improve through a loading exposure.
The medial malleolar rule-out is effectively answered by the trajectory** — though the bone palpation
test was never explicitly reported, so it is answered by inference rather than by test.

**⚠️ THIS IS A LEFT-LEG INJURY AND IS UNRELATED TO THE CLOSED COMPENSATION FLAG**, which was entirely
right-sided (posteromedial right knee 19 Aug; dorsal right 4th/5th MTP 23 and 30 Aug). The 6 Sep
evidence that closed that flag says nothing about this one, and must not be applied to it.

## Three corrections this week — two of them mine

**1. The Tuesday Barry's flag (withdrawn 6 Sep).** The 30 Aug plan said "walking lunges, no
plyometrics" for Tue 8 Sep. **The athlete said he would get injured and asked for a recovery week.
He was right** — the flag had treated attendance as given and negotiated only the plyometrics, when
the correct call was that a lower-body class does not happen 48 h after a goal-race half marathon at
all.

**2. The hop test (withdrawn 13 Sep).** It entered the repo on 25 Aug as a **bone stress-reaction
screen** and was wrongly re-purposed on 12 Sep as a **tendon readiness gate**. Ten unpractised
maximal hops on a tendon unloaded for six days is a novel stimulus; **tightness was the expected
response, not a finding, and it should not have held Sunday's run.** The athlete challenged the test
rather than the ankle, and the challenge was upheld.

**3. The missing loading programme (issued 13 Sep) — the most significant of the three.** The 9 and
12 Sep updates prescribed rest, no cycling, and a battery of tests. **They prescribed no load at
all.** Tendons remodel in response to graded load and lose stiffness without it, so **a week of pure
rest plausibly left the tendon less ready than it started.** Complete rest is the correct treatment
for bone stress and for illness; it is the wrong treatment for tendinopathy.

**The pattern worth carrying forward: this repo's injury reflexes were built on five illness episodes
and a bone-stress rule-out, and both default to abstinence. Applied to a tendon, that default is
actively counterproductive.** Tendon problems get graded load, not rest.

2. **The lower-body Barry's gap reached 21 days (25 Aug → 15 Sep)** — exactly the 31 Mar VMO
