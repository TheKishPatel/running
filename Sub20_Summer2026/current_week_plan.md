## 🔴 TUESDAY'S BARRY'S IS OFF — and the earlier flag was wrong

**The 30 Aug flag said "Barry's Tue 8 Sep — walking lunges, no plyometrics." That was the
wrong call and the athlete caught it.** It treated attendance as given and negotiated only
the plyometrics. **The right call is that a lower-body class does not happen 48 hours after
a goal-race half marathon at all** — walking lunges are still heavy eccentric loading on
legs carrying 21.1 km of race pounding, and muscle damage from an effort like that takes
5–7 days to clear, with strength markers down for about a week.

**This is not the `CLAUDE.md` rule being broken.** That rule says Barry's is never dropped
**to protect a running session** — and nothing here is protecting a run. This is protecting
recovery from a race, which is a different thing. Two parts of the same rule set apply
instead:

- **"Upper-body Barry's: attend freely, no restrictions."** ✅ **Tuesday upper body is
  genuinely free and costs the legs nothing.** Take it if you want the session and the
  routine. **Optional, not prescribed.**
- **"Only modify plyometric elements if legs are already heavily fatigued from a hard
  Sunday long run."** A goal-race half marathon is more than a hard long run, and
  modification is not a big enough lever here.

### 🚩 The gap this creates, and how it gets closed

**Last lower-body session: 25 Aug.** Skipping 8 Sep means the next realistic one is
**Tue 15 Sep — a 21-day gap.** That is exactly the 31 Mar VMO precedent (21-day gap →
jumping lunges → deconditioning injury), so it does not get ignored:

- **Lower body returns Tue 15 Sep**, conditions permitting.
- **Walking lunges for the first 2–3 sessions back, and all plyometrics modified on the
  first one** — jumping lunges, jump squats, box jumps. Per the standing gap protocol in
  `CLAUDE.md`.
- **That is the trade being made deliberately:** a 21-day gap costs some lower-body
  conditioning and is recoverable in 2–3 sessions. A lower-body class on 48-hour race legs
  risks an injury that costs the October block entirely.

### ❌ THE HOP TEST IS WITHDRAWN FROM THE GATE — the athlete's challenge was correct

**Sunday was held because ten hops gave tightness. That should not have blocked the run.**

**The hop test entered this repo on 25 Aug as a BONE stress-reaction screen** — *"point-tender on the
bone, painful on hopping"*. **That is its only valid use.** On 12 Sep it was re-purposed as a tendon
readiness gate, which it is not fit for: ten unpractised maximal hops on a tendon that had carried no
load for six days **is itself a novel stimulus, and tightness is the expected response rather than a
finding.** Running is a repeated *submaximal* cycle; hopping is repeated *maximal* impact. **Passing
it was never a prerequisite for 4 km at 6:00/km on grass.**

**Retained only as:** sharp, point-specific pain **on the bone** when hopping = red flag. **Tightness
or general ache = not a finding, does not block a run.**

**And the broader correction — this flag had no loading plan, which is why the tendon felt tight.**
Rest, no cycling and a battery of tests were prescribed; **load was not.** Tendons remodel in
response to graded load and lose stiffness without it. **A tendon loading programme is now issued in
`current_status.md` and starts today** — isometric holds daily, slow heavy single-leg heel raises
every other day, band inversion added after. **The heel raise is both the test and the treatment.
That is why it, and not hopping, is the gate.**
