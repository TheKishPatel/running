## HR data is invalid (sensor undercount)

Disregard the 143–154 BPM readings:
- Km1 was **4:11/km** (sub-21 pace) at a claimed **146 BPM**. Easy running at 5:30/km already sits at 140 BPM — running 80 sec/km faster cannot be +6 beats. Physiologically impossible.
- **Identical to race day 8 Mar 2026**, where the watch read 146–153 against a true ~175–182. Optical sensors undercount in races (dry/cold start, sweat, fit).
- **Action for the July goal race: use a chest strap** so race HR is real.

Read this session via **power + pace + RPE**, not HR.

## What the power shows

- **Km1–2: 293–294W** — genuine threshold/low-VO2max power (tempo was historically 260–280W, intervals 290–310W). The opening was a real effort, not a jog.
- **Km3–5: 256–264W** — downshift to steady/tempo. Pace ballooned to 4:30–4:41 as the heat bit and he deliberately eased.

So: opened honest at sub-21 pace, then backed off to a controlled steady effort. Exactly "took it easy."

## Why this is the right execution

~30°C + 6 days off a chest infection. Holding race pace was never realistic, and grinding it would have risked a relapse for a meaningless hot-day number. Easing off protected the real prize (the July goal race). Correct call.

## The useful signal

He opened at **sub-21 pace and threshold power six days after a chest infection.** The leg speed and aerobic engine came through the illness intact. That is the only clean read available, and it's positive for the build.

## Not concluded
