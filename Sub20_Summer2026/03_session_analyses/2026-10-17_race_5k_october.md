## Finding 1 — the pushed section ran 10+ bpm high and faded 19 sec/km

| | Pace | HR | Power |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 Sep threshold, km3–5 (healthy) | 4:17.7 | 169 | 276 W |
| **17 Oct km2–3 (race, post-illness)** | **4:28.5** | **179** | **274 W** |

**Same power, 11 sec/km slower, +10 bpm.** Same offset as Tue 13's easy run (+14 to +23 bpm at the same power vs 4 Oct).
The engine hadn't come back. km1 4:12 matches the 4:10 plan, but 293 W then fell to 260 W by km3. A fade like that
is a capacity limit, not a pacing mistake.

Power held better than pace (287 W at 4:26). Possible course or surface cost (elevation not readable from the screenshot).

## Finding 2 — this has happened before, almost identically

| | km1 | km2 | km3 | Context |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **27 Jun tune-up** | 4:11 / 293 W | 4:21 / 294 W | 4:30 / 262 W | 6 days post chest infection, ~30°C |
| **17 Oct race** | 4:12 / 293 W | 4:26 / 287 W | 4:31 / 260 W | ~9 days post cold, 13 days barely running |

**Two races run under the same conditions produced the same curve.** Racing within ~10 days of an illness costs about
20 sec/km by km3, whatever the underlying fitness. The pattern is now documented twice.
