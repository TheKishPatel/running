## What actually happened: a 16 sec/km positive split that the fitness absorbed

| Segment | Pace | HR | Power | Efficiency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **km 1–10** | **4:35.2** | 166.6 | **256.9 W** | **1.542 w/bpm** |
| **km 11–20** | **4:51.3** | 174.1 | **244.6 W** | **1.405 w/bpm** |
| **Change** | **+16.1 sec/km** | **+7.5 bpm** | **−12.3 W** | **−8.9%** |

**This is a real fade, not a course artifact.** The distinction matters and power settles it:
if the second half had simply been hillier, **power would have held and pace would have
dropped.** Power fell 12.3 W while heart rate rose 7.5 bpm. That is decoupling — the
back half was paid for out of the front half.

**8.9% against 6.2% on the 18 km (23 Aug) and 4.3% on the 12 km (2 Aug).** Higher, but
this was a race at 168 avg for 99 minutes rather than a Zone 2 long run, so a larger
number is expected. It is not an alarming figure; it is the size of the bill for the open.

## The three things that say he was fitter than the result

**1. There was a finishing gear, so this was not a wall.** km21 ran **4:42 at 254 W — the
highest power of the entire second half** — and the closing 413 m ran **4:36**. An athlete
who has actually emptied the tank cannot do that. The fade between km 15 and 20 was
pacing arithmetic, not glycogen.

**2. He held 4:35/km for 10 km at 166.6 bpm** — *below* the 168–176 threshold band. The
first half of this race was run at sub-threshold heart rate at a pace 15 sec/km faster than
the race target.

**3. The final read understated him, and that is now visible.** 30 Aug produced 4:49.7/km
at 157.7 bpm and the verdict table read it as "1:42 confirmed." **The race ran 7 sec/km
faster than that for 3.5 times the duration.** The read was taken on a five-waking night,
48 h after a maximal 5 km, as a 6 km block inside a 14 km run — every one of those pushed
the number conservative.

**Estimate: evenly paced at ~4:43 this is the same time with a far better second half; run
to a disciplined 4:45 open it is most likely 1:38:30–1:39:00.** The gain from better pacing
here is worth roughly 30–60 seconds, not five minutes — the open cost something, but the
result is close to the athlete's honest current ceiling.

## Course reading — two splits that are gradient, not fitness

**km7 at 4:07/km is not a fitness datapoint. Do not treat it as one.** It ran 22 sec/km
faster than km6 for **9 W more** — at that pace, 22 sec/km is worth ~20–25 W. A gap that
size at equal power is a downhill, and km6 (4:29 at 258 W) is the top of the same descent.

**km4 is the mirror image:** 4:39 at **274 W**, the highest power of the race, 1 sec/km
*slower* than km3 at 247 W. That is a climb.

**km20 at 5:00 (238 W) followed by km21 at 4:42 (254 W)** is the same pattern at the end —
a rise, then the run home.

**The course is undulating and the raw splits overstate both the mid-race surge and the
late fade.** Power is the honest signal throughout.

## What this means for the October 5K

**The volume experiment is answered, and that was the campaign's real open question.** The
5K campaign died at 4:00/km with threshold never below 4:20 and volume at 20–25 km/week;
low volume was named as the untested ceiling and this block finally tested it. **21.1 km at
4:43/km off a six-week block settles it: the aerobic base is no longer the limiter.**

**But the half does not predict sub-20, and it would be dishonest to present it as if it
did.** Riegel from 1:39:32 gives a **5K of 21:38** — *slower* than the existing 20:59 PB.
Reverse Riegel from 20:59 gives a half of **1:36:32**, three minutes faster than he ran.
**Read together: his 5K speed is still ahead of his endurance, and the half was raced with
a positive split. The two facts point the same way — this was an endurance result, not a
speed one.**

**The gap to sub-20 is exactly where the block left it: threshold.** 4:20/km (2 Jul) →
4:23/km (6 Aug) → nothing, across three consecutive compromised Thursdays. **That bill is
now due.** The 27 Aug unplanned 5 km (22:28 off no warm-up, km2 at 4:14) projects to
~21:15–21:45 rested and evenly paced, and that is the honest starting line.

**Four weeks of sharpening closes some of ~75–105 seconds, not all of it. Sub-20:30 and a
PB are the realistic October targets; sub-20 is a stretch that requires the threshold work
this block never got.**
