## Verdict: completed, under-pressed

**4:23/km against 4:18 prescribed — 5 sec/km slow — at 169 bpm, the bottom of the 168–176 band.**
The ease-threshold was 178. He finished with roughly 7 bpm of unused headroom and a session
that never asked a hard question.

## Threshold is flat versus 2 July, not improved

This is the finding that matters, and it is not the one the block was built on.

| | 2 Jul | **6 Aug** |
|---|---|---|
| Main set | 4 km @ **4:20/km** | 4 km @ **4:23/km** |
| HR | 156 / 171 / 175 / 179 (mean ~170) | **169** |
| Power | 271–281 w | **272–275 w** |

**3 sec/km slower, 1 bpm lower, same power, five weeks apart.** At matched power the
comparison is tighter still: 2 Jul's T2 was 4:23 @ 171 bpm / 272 w — today's *entire 4 km*
averaged 4:23 @ 169 bpm / 273 w.

Holding one rep's pace across the whole set is a small durability gain and it is real. **But
the threshold pace itself has not moved since 2 July.** 4:18 was an optimistic prescription
written off a 12-day quality layoff, in a week containing a resting-HR scare and four
consecutive short nights.

## What went right

**Execution shape was correct.** 4:26 → 4:23 → 4:21, HR 168 → 169 → 173, power 272 → 273 → 275.
Descending pace, no early spike, controlled throughout. Unlike 2 Jul — which ended 4:13 @ 179,
fully emptied — he finished this one with something left.

**The structured workout worked on its first outing.** Because the session was built on the
watch, the Intervals screen returned a clean Work aggregate despite the 1.5 km warm-up doing
exactly the damage predicted to the km splits (km2 = 5:06 @ 146, km6 = 5:13 @ 162 — both
meaningless as written). **Structured workouts are now standard for every multi-intensity
session.**

## Behavioural note — the first under-target session of the campaign

Every prior pace flag was overspeed: 23 Jul 400s, 30 Jul strides, 1 Aug easy run. **This is the
first session run slower than prescribed**, and it reads as over-correction after three weeks
of slow-down instruction.

**The distinction to hold:** pace discipline governs **easy runs** and the **opening rep** of a
quality set. On the *body* of a threshold set the prescribed pace **is** the target and HR is
the ceiling, not the aim. At 169 with a 178 ease-threshold, there was room and it wasn't taken.
