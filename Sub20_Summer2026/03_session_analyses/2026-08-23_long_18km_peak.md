## 3. Aerobic cost — the strongest endurance evidence of the campaign

**km3–18 (16 km) at 5:45.4/km @ 133.1 bpm and 209.6 w — efficiency 1.575 w/bpm.**

Against the 16 Aug body of the run (km2–13, 5:42 @ 138.6, 212 w, **1.529**): **3 sec/km slower,
but 5.5 bpm lower, over 4 km further. Efficiency up 3.0%.** Sixteen kilometres today cost less
per beat than twelve did a week ago.

Power was **flat at 197–215 w for the entire run** with cadence pinned at 163 spm. Total Zone 1+2
= 100% of 1:42:54 of zone data.

**Post-workout 129 → 96 → 90 = 33 bpm in the first minute, 39 over two**, off an already-low 129
after 1 h 44. In line with the best long-run traces in the log (2 Aug 138→98→90; 16 Aug 129→94→90)
despite being the longest run ever recorded here.

**Honest counterweight — decoupling 6.2%.** EF fell 1.628 (km3–10) → 1.526 (km11–18), i.e. **above
the 5% threshold**, against 4.3% on the 12 km of 2 Aug. Longer runs decouple more and 1 h 44 in
late-August London carries thermal load, so this is not a red flag — **but it is drift, and it is
the one number in this session that is not a personal best.** Note that the drift was entirely
**HR at constant power and pace** — nothing faded except the heart rate's efficiency, which is
ordinary cardiac drift rather than a pacing failure.

## 4. ⚠️ NEW FLAG — fatigue-dependent compensation, last 4 km

**Reported: pain/fatigue in the knee ligament–tendon on the OPPOSITE side to the injury, and in the
RIGHT FOOT, during the last 4 km. Next morning: no pain, general fatigue in both legs only.**

*(Assumption to confirm: the original injury was posteromedial RIGHT knee, so "opposite side"
is read here as the LATERAL right knee — same leg, other aspect. Both reported symptoms are on
the right. If it was in fact the LEFT knee, this reads differently and the entry needs revising.)*

**This is the textbook signature of gait compensation around a healing medial structure:** load
redistributed to the lateral structures and the foot of the same leg, appearing **only under
fatigue**, in **exactly the last-quarter window** identified as the risk on 22 Aug. Two features
keep it a yellow flag rather than a red one:

1. **The original injury stayed completely silent** — the medial side, four days post-resolution,
   said nothing across 18 km. That is the structure that mattered and it held.
2. **Full resolution inside 24 h**, with only bilateral general fatigue remaining. A structural
   problem does not clear overnight; a load-distribution problem does.

**But it is new, it is load-dependent, and Week 5 puts race pace back in twice** (Thu 8 km
progressive with the last 4 at 4:50; Sun 14 km with the middle 6 at 4:50). **Race pace means
higher force, and higher force through an altered gait is how a compensation becomes an injury.**

**Actions:**
- **Tue 25 Aug Barry's — modify the plyometrics.** Jumping lunges resumed only on 18 Aug and this
  is 48 h after the longest run ever run, with a fresh compensation signal. Walking lunges, no jump
  squats, no box jumps. Barry's still happens — the plyometrics are the negotiable part.
- **Report the right foot specifically before Thursday.** Location matters: arch, heel, forefoot,
  or the outside edge each mean something different.
- **In Week 5's race-pace segments, any return of lateral knee or foot symptoms ends the segment** —
  drop to easy pace and finish the run aerobically. Two weeks out, a race-pace rep is worth nothing
  against a compromised start line.
- **Escalate to a physio** if the lateral knee or foot recurs on a second run, or persists beyond
  24 h on any occasion.
